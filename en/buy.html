How buying works

New builds and resale homes follow different steps

  1. Get an Agreement in Principle

    If you need a mortgage, get one from a lender or broker first. It shows sellers you're serious and strengthens your offer.

  2. Search and view

    If you're abroad, a consultant views for you, films and photographs it, and reports on the surroundings.

  3. Offer and Memorandum of Sale

    Once a price is agreed, the memorandum records the price and the property details.

  4. Survey and mortgage approvalabout 2–3 months

    A surveyor checks the inside, outside, garage and garden; issues can be raised with the seller or used to renegotiate. The lender approves based on valuation and affordability.

  5. Exchange and completion

    Solicitors draft the contract and you pay about 10% on exchange. Once everything is in place you pay the balance and stamp duty and collect the keys.

  1. Reservation agreement

    Choose a plot and pay a reservation fee, from a few hundred to a few thousand pounds depending on the developer.

  2. Instruct a solicitor and exchange

    Sign the contract and usually pay about 10% of the price as a deposit.

  3. Mortgageabout 6 months before handover

    If you need a mortgage, we introduce a broker to find a suitable product.

  4. Snagging inspection

    An inspector checks the home before handover and lists anything the developer must fix.

  5. Completion and keys

    Pay the balance, stamp duty and fees, and move in.

Tax when buying

Stamp Duty Land Tax

Stamp duty is charged in bands of the price. First-time buyers, additional homes and non-residents pay different rates. These are the rates for homes in England.

Portion of the priceStandardAdditional home
Up to £125,0000%5%
£125,001 – £250,0002%7%
£250,001 – £925,0005%10%
£925,001 – £1.5M10%15%
Over £1.5M12%17%

Rates in force from April 2025, for guidance only. Check the latest on GOV.UK.

First-time buyers: nothing on the first £300,000 and 5% on £300,001–£500,000. Above £500,000, standard rates apply.

Non-resident surcharge: an extra 2% on every band if you spent fewer than 183 days in the UK in the 12 months before buying.

Council Tax: paid yearly by whoever lives in the home, based on the property's band and area.

FAQ

About buying in the UK

Stamp duty, tenure and extra costs come up every time.

Can't find your question? Ask us
I moved on a BN(O) visa. Do I pay the non-resident stamp duty surcharge?

The extra 2% depends on how long you have been in the UK, not on your visa or passport. If you spent fewer than 183 days in the UK in the 12 months before buying, it usually applies. Families who buy soon after arriving should watch for this; in some cases it can be reclaimed later. Confirm your situation with your solicitor.

What's the difference between freehold and leasehold?

Freehold means you own the building and the land outright. Leasehold means you own the home for a fixed term, often 99 to 999 years, and pay ground rent, service charges and building insurance. Leasehold homes cost less but usually grow less in value, and short leases are harder to sell or mortgage, so always check the remaining term.

Apart from stamp duty, what other costs are there?

Typical costs include solicitor fees of about £1,000–2,000, a survey at about £600–850 and a Homebuyer Report at about £400–650 (strongly advised for older homes), plus mortgage fees, removals and insurance. These are guides only and vary by firm, area and price.

I own property in Hong Kong. What UK tax applies?

Once you are UK tax resident, the UK taxes worldwide income and gains. Selling overseas property may mean Capital Gains Tax, and rent from a let property may be subject to Income Tax. We can introduce accountants who know Hong Kong clients; it's best to plan before you buy.

YouTubers say MK4, 5, 8 and 10 are the best parts of Milton Keynes. True?

Those areas are popular overall, but every area has streets you would avoid. The most common mistake is buying from online photos alone, then finding the home doesn't match and won't let. Someone needs to see it in person.
